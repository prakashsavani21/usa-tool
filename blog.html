How to Calculate a Percentage Easily

Percentages are used in everyday situations such as discounts, test scores, price changes, taxes and financial calculations. A percentage simply represents a value out of 100.

The basic percentage formula

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

For example, if you answered 45 questions correctly out of 60, divide 45 by 60 and multiply the result by 100. This gives a score of 75%.

Finding a percentage of a number

To find a percentage of a number, convert the percentage to a decimal and multiply it by the number. For example, 20% of 150 is 0.20 × 150 = 30.

Where percentages are useful

Percentages can help you compare values, understand price changes, calculate savings and measure results. They are especially useful when the original values are different sizes but you want a common way to compare them.

How Loan Payments Are Calculated

A loan payment depends mainly on the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the length of the loan. Understanding these factors can make it easier to compare different repayment options.

Loan amount

The loan amount is the principal you borrow. A larger principal generally results in a larger monthly payment when the interest rate and loan term remain the same.

Interest rate

The interest rate determines how much interest is charged on the outstanding balance. A higher rate generally increases both the monthly payment and the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Loan term

The loan term is the amount of time allowed for repayment. A longer term can reduce the required monthly payment, but it can also increase the total interest paid.

Monthly payment

For a typical fixed-rate installment loan, the payment is calculated using the principal, periodic interest rate and number of payments. The payment is structured so the loan can be fully repaid by the end of the term.

Simple Ways to Calculate a Discount

Discounts are commonly shown as percentages. Calculating the discount amount first makes it easy to find the final price.

Discount amount formula

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount Percentage ÷ 100)

Final price formula

Final Price = Original Price − Discount Amount

For example, if an item costs $80 and has a 25% discount, the discount is $20. The final price is therefore $60.

Why calculate the discount separately?

Breaking the calculation into two steps helps you clearly see how much money is saved and what amount you will actually pay. This is useful when comparing multiple offers.
