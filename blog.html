IRS Urges Workers to Do a Paycheck Tax Checkup in 2026

The IRS is encouraging workers and employers to review paycheck withholding and payroll tax responsibilities during National Payroll Week. A quick check can help workers make sure their federal income tax withholding is broadly aligned with their expected tax situation.

Why a paycheck checkup matters

Federal income tax is generally collected throughout the year through withholding from paychecks. If too little tax is withheld, a taxpayer may face a larger balance when filing a return. If too much is withheld, the taxpayer may receive a larger refund instead of having access to that money during the year.

What to review

Workers can review their recent pay stubs, withholding information and personal circumstances that may affect their tax situation. Changes such as a new job, marriage, a change in dependents or other major life events can affect the amount of tax that should be withheld.

Where to get official guidance

The IRS recommends using its official tax resources when checking withholding and payroll tax responsibilities. Taxpayers with complicated situations may also want to consult a qualified tax professional.

Source: IRS, “IRS reminder: National Payroll Week is time for a paycheck checkup,” September 4, 2026.

2026 Tax Extension: What Filers Should Know About the October 15 Deadline

Taxpayers who requested an extension to file their 2025 federal income tax return generally have until October 15, 2026, to file. An extension gives additional time to file a return, but it does not generally extend the time to pay tax that was already due.

Who should pay attention?

This deadline is especially relevant to taxpayers who requested an extension during the 2026 filing season and have not yet submitted their federal return. Waiting until the deadline can leave less time to correct errors or gather missing documents.

Filing and payment are different

An extension to file is not the same as an extension to pay. Taxpayers who owed tax may still have interest and applicable penalties if the required amount was not paid by the original deadline.

Use official IRS resources

The IRS recommends that taxpayers who received an extension file as soon as they are ready rather than waiting until the last day. Eligible taxpayers can also use IRS online services and available filing resources.

Source: IRS, “IRS reminder: Extension filers can use IRS Free File this summer,” August 26, 2026.

Fed Rate Outlook in September 2026: What Higher Rates Could Mean for Borrowers

Interest-rate expectations have shifted in early September 2026 after a stronger-than-expected August U.S. jobs report. What changed?

Reuters reported on September 7 that UBS expects two 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate increases in 2026, one in September and another in December. The change followed an August jobs report showing 162,000 jobs added and an unemployment rate of 4.1%.

Why rates matter to consumers

Higher interest rates can increase borrowing costs for products such as adjustable-rate loans, some credit products and new mortgages. What borrowers can do

How to Calculate a Percentage Easily

Percentages are used in everyday situations such as discounts, test scores, price changes, taxes and financial calculations. A percentage simply represents a value out of 100.

The basic percentage formula

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

For example, if you answered 45 questions correctly out of 60, divide 45 by 60 and multiply the result by 100. This gives a score of 75%.

Finding a percentage of a number

To find a percentage of a number, convert the percentage to a decimal and multiply it by the number. For example, 20% of 150 is 0.20 × 150 = 30.

Where percentages are useful

Percentages can help you compare values, understand price changes, calculate savings and measure results. They are especially useful when the original values are different sizes but you want a common way to compare them.

How Loan Payments Are Calculated

A loan payment depends mainly on the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the length of the loan. Understanding these factors can make it easier to compare different repayment options.

Loan amount

The loan amount is the principal you borrow. A larger principal generally results in a larger monthly payment when the interest rate and loan term remain the same.

Interest rate

The interest rate determines how much interest is charged on the outstanding balance. A higher rate generally increases both the monthly payment and the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Loan term

The loan term is the amount of time allowed for repayment. A longer term can reduce the required monthly payment, but it can also increase the total interest paid.

Monthly payment

For a typical fixed-rate installment loan, the payment is calculated using the principal, periodic interest rate and number of payments. The payment is structured so the loan can be fully repaid by the end of the term.

Simple Ways to Calculate a Discount

Discounts are commonly shown as percentages. Calculating the discount amount first makes it easy to find the final price.

Discount amount formula

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount Percentage ÷ 100)

Final price formula

Final Price = Original Price − Discount Amount

For example, if an item costs $80 and has a 25% discount, the discount is $20. The final price is therefore $60.

Why calculate the discount separately?

Breaking the calculation into two steps helps you clearly see how much money is saved and what amount you will actually pay. This is useful when comparing multiple offers.

How to Calculate Percentage Increase and Decrease

Percentage increase and decrease calculations are useful when comparing an original value with a new value. They can help you understand changes in prices, sales, scores, measurements and other numbers.

Percentage increase formula

Percentage Increase = ((New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

For example, if a price increases from $50 to $60, the increase is $10. Divide $10 by $50 and multiply by 100. The percentage increase is 20%.

Percentage decrease formula

Percentage Decrease = ((Original Value − New Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

If a price falls from $80 to $60, the decrease is $20. Dividing $20 by $80 and multiplying by 100 gives a 25% decrease.

Why the original value matters

The original value is used as the starting point for the comparison. Using the new value instead can produce a different percentage, so it is important to identify which number is the original value.

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

Interest is the amount earned or charged on money over time. Simple interest and compound interest use different methods, so understanding the difference can make financial calculations easier to follow.

Simple interest

Simple Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

With simple interest, the interest is calculated on the original principal. For example, if the principal is $1,000, the annual rate is 5%, and the time is 2 years, the simple interest is $100.

Compound interest

Compound interest allows previously accumulated interest to become part of the balance used for later interest calculations. This means the balance can grow faster over time.

Compound Amount = Principal × (1 + Rate ÷ Number of Periods)^(Number of Periods × Time)

What is the main difference?

Simple interest is based on the original principal, while compound interest can include accumulated interest in future calculations. The difference can become more noticeable over longer periods.

Why the comparison is useful

Understanding both methods can help when reading financial examples, comparing savings scenarios or checking how interest calculations work.

How to Calculate an Average

An average is a simple way to represent a group of numbers with one value. The most common type is the arithmetic mean, which is calculated by adding the values and dividing the total by the number of values.

Average formula

Average = Sum of Values ÷ Number of Values

For example, consider the numbers 10, 20, 30 and 40. Their sum is 100. Since there are four values, the average is 100 ÷ 4 = 25.

Why averages are useful

Averages can help summarize test scores, prices, measurements, ratings and other groups of numbers. They provide a quick way to understand the general level of a dataset.

Averages and unusual values

A very large or very small value can affect the arithmetic mean. When a dataset contains unusual values, it can be useful to look at the individual numbers as well rather than relying only on the average.

2026 Sales Tax Rates by State: What Shoppers Need to Know

Sales tax in the United States can vary significantly depending on the state, city, county and type of purchase. The state rate is only part of the final tax you may see at checkout because local governments can add their own sales taxes.

How sales tax works

When you buy a taxable item, the sales tax is generally calculated as a percentage of the taxable purchase price. The total rate may combine a state rate with local taxes.

Sales Tax = Taxable Price × Sales Tax Rate

2026 state-level rates

For 2026, California has the highest state-level sales tax rate at 7.25%. Colorado has the lowest non-zero state-level rate at 2.9%. Five states have no statewide sales tax: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon.

Why your actual rate can be different

Two shoppers in the same state can sometimes pay different combined sales tax rates because local jurisdictions may impose additional taxes. For that reason, a state-level rate should not automatically be treated as the exact rate at a particular store or address.

How to estimate a purchase

If a taxable purchase is $100 and the combined tax rate is 8%, the estimated sales tax is $8 and the total is $108.

Which States Have No Sales Tax in 2026?

If you are comparing shopping costs across the United States, one common question is which states do not impose a statewide sales tax. In 2026, five states have no statewide sales tax.

The five states with no statewide sales tax

The states are Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon.

Does that always mean zero tax at checkout?

No. A state having no statewide sales tax does not necessarily mean every purchase is completely free of sales tax or other taxes. Alaska, for example, allows local governments to impose local sales taxes.

What about the other four states?

Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon do not have a statewide general sales tax. However, shoppers should still consider other costs, special taxes, fees or product-specific rules when comparing the total cost of a purchase.

Why sales tax is only one part of the picture

A lower sales tax rate does not automatically make a state cheaper overall. Housing, income taxes, property taxes, local taxes and everyday prices can all affect a household's total expenses.

2026 Sales Tax on Groceries: What Changed?

Grocery sales tax rules are not the same across the United States. Some states exempt groceries from their general sales tax, some apply a reduced rate, and others continue to tax certain grocery purchases.

Why grocery taxes are different

States can define taxable food differently, and a grocery item that is exempt in one state may be taxed in another. Local taxes can also affect the final amount in some places.

Important 2026 changes

Illinois eliminated its 1% state sales tax on food items effective January 1, 2026. However, local jurisdictions can still impose their own taxes on food.

Other states have also changed grocery-tax policies in recent years. Arkansas, Kansas and Oklahoma are among the states that have eliminated certain statewide grocery taxes, although local taxes and product-specific rules can still matter.

Why shoppers should check local rules

A grocery receipt may include different tax treatment depending on the item and where the purchase is made. Prepared food, restaurant meals and other products may be treated differently from ordinary groceries.

How to estimate the tax

If a taxable grocery purchase is $150 and the applicable tax rate is 2%, the estimated tax is $3. The total would be $153.

Because food-tax rules can be complicated, use a local or state tax source when you need an exact answer for a specific purchase.

Data note: Grocery tax rules can change and local taxes may apply even when a state-level grocery tax has been removed. This article is based on 2026 information from the Tax Foundation and current reporting on state grocery-tax policies.
